# Equipment, Uniforms & Safety

This section sets out {{leagueAbbrev}}'s policies and procedures for player and field
safety, safety certification of volunteers, weather stoppages, and the
purchase, care, distribution, and security of league equipment and uniforms.
The website pages linked below are the current source of truth; agendas and
league emails provide operational detail and history.

## 1. Safety Program (ASAP)

{{leagueAbbrev}} operates a written safety plan under Little League's "As Safe As
Possible" (ASAP) program.

- Each year, all coaches must attend the league's ASAP training. Coaches who
  cannot attend are required to watch the posted ASAP plan video instead
  (source: website coaches-corner/asap-plan-coach-training).
- Every team must have at least one coach attend the annual Coaches Safety
  Meeting & Training Session (Tee Ball through Majors) in order to meet
  Little League safety requirements; all coaches are asked to attend
  (source: website coaches-corner).
- Coaches must carry a copy of the ASAP plan to all games and practices, and
  that copy must also include completed medical release forms for all players
  on the team (source: website coaches-corner/asap-plan-coach-training).
- The ASAP plan is maintained online and coaches who did not receive a copy
  should contact the league at {{infoEmail}}
  (source: website coaches-corner/asap-plan-coach-training).

The Safety Officer owns the safety program. The role's duties are to ensure
the fields are safe for play (and to request/coordinate repairs), prepare and
stock medical (first aid) kits, file the annual safety report with Little
League International, prepare the coaches' handbooks containing the safety
plan/report, and file reports when a player is injured
(source: email Safety Officer). The Safety Officer is a board position filled
annually at the organizational meeting (e.g., Bobbi Jo Doucette for 2025)
(source: agenda 2025-02-24).

## 2. Certified Volunteer Requirement

To be in the snack shack, on the field when children are present, or at a
practice facility, a person must be a "Certified Volunteer" and wear their
league-issued Certified Volunteer Badge (source: website
frequently-asked-questions/certified-volunteer). Certification is required
for the safety of players and to meet the requirements of the league's
insurer, and volunteers are asked to complete it as soon as possible
(source: website frequently-asked-questions/certified-volunteer).

Certification is a three-step process (source: website
frequently-asked-questions/certified-volunteer):

1. Register through the league registration system.
2. Complete a background check submitted online through "JDP / JD Palatine."
3. Complete Little League's Abuse Awareness (Safe Sport) training, selecting
   "{{leagueName}}" when creating the account.

Badge issuance procedures:

- Badges are issued as the league sees completions on its certification
  tracking system, and are brought to {{indoorVenue}} at 5:30 on practice days for
  pickup; volunteers are notified when their badge is ready (source: website
  frequently-asked-questions/certified-volunteer).
- To be valid for a badge, a volunteer's record must show "Passed" in the
  Verification Status column and a "Y" or "M" in the Abuse Awareness Training
  Completed column (source: email {{leagueAbbrev}} - Certified Volunteer Badges).
- The badge requirement was formally adopted by the board; the Safe Sport /
  Abuse Awareness training badge was designed and ordered by the president
  with the design approved by the board (source: agenda 2024-02-04). Badges
  were described on the website as "a new addition this year" for that season
  (source: website frequently-asked-questions/certified-volunteer).

## 3. Weather Safety: Rainouts

The league's goal is to get as much safe, fun play as possible, and coaches
have discretion to cancel based on cold and field conditions (source: website
frequently-asked-questions/rainout-lightning-guidelines).

- Cancel if it is too cold for the level or if the field is too wet to play
  on. Do not cancel due to a threat of rain until one hour before game time,
  since the weather may miss the fields. Tee Ball and Coach Pitch may cancel
  earlier because of the short duration of their events (source: website
  frequently-asked-questions/rainout-lightning-guidelines).
- Temperature guidance: for Tee Ball and Coach Pitch, give it a shot if it is
  above 45 degrees and not windy; below 45 and drizzly or windy is likely not
  worth it. For AA (player pitch), AAA (minors), and Majors, generally give it
  a shot at 40 degrees and above (source: website
  frequently-asked-questions/rainout-lightning-guidelines).
- Play may continue in a drizzle with no lightning or thunder; coaches should
  bring towels to dry off baseballs (source: website
  frequently-asked-questions/rainout-lightning-guidelines).
- Field conditions: if a field is soaked with standing water, cancel. If you
  leave deep mud-prints when walking on it, the field is not safe to play and
  could be damaged. {{townC}}'s Mill Street field drains poorly and often takes
  12-24 hours to dry out after heavy rain (source: website
  frequently-asked-questions/rainout-lightning-guidelines).
- Games and practices are cancelled through GameChanger by editing the event
  and canceling it in the app; for games, coordinate with the opposing coach
  before canceling (source: website
  frequently-asked-questions/rainout-lightning-guidelines).

## 4. Weather Safety: Lightning

Managers must be aware of the dangers of lightning. The general Little League
policy applies: "If you hear it – clear it" (clear the field for at least 30
minutes) and "If you see it – flee it" (leave the field / game ends)
(source: website frequently-asked-questions/rainout-lightning-guidelines).

- With the first sound of thunder or visible lightning, clear the field
  (source: website frequently-asked-questions/rainout-lightning-guidelines).
- When lightning is determined to be within 10 miles, play is suspended
  immediately. All players, managers, coaches, and volunteers must be cleared
  from the dugout and all external field areas — moving to a large enclosed
  building, or to metal-top cars with windows rolled up if no building is
  available (source: website
  frequently-asked-questions/rainout-lightning-guidelines).
- Play resumes only on an "all clear" from the umpire, which may be issued
  only when all lightning/thunder activity has moved beyond a 10-mile radius
  and none is expected to move in, or when at least 30 minutes have elapsed
  since the last visible lightning strike or thunder (source: website
  frequently-asked-questions/rainout-lightning-guidelines).
- The umpire may cancel the game as a rainout at their discretion. On
  cancellation, ensure all players are accounted for and leave with approved
  persons (source: website
  frequently-asked-questions/rainout-lightning-guidelines).
- If someone is struck by lightning, call 911, begin first aid/CPR as
  necessary, and move the victim to a safer place; lightning victims carry no
  charge and are safe to handle (source: website
  frequently-asked-questions/rainout-lightning-guidelines).

## 5. First Aid, AED & Emergency Preparedness

- First aid kits are stocked and placed at the fields; the Safety Officer is
  responsible for preparing and stocking medical kits and is asked to check
  and stock kits once the fields are set up (source: email First Aid Kits;
  source: email Safety Officer).
- The board voted to offer a Heartsaver CPR / First Aid / AED training class,
  half-subsidized by the league, on a first-come first-served basis to
  coaches and then other registered volunteers, up to 24 participants at {{amount}}
  per person (source: agenda 2023-02-26).
- The board has pursued placement of AED devices at fields, weighing device
  maintenance requirements (favoring models needing only monthly/yearly
  self-tests), battery life of 2-5 years, and device replacement every 5-8
  years (source: agenda 2023-02-26).

## 6. Player Safety On-Field Conduct

- Using an improper bat is an immediate ejection from the game, and the coach
  is ejected as well; a coach is additionally suspended one game if an illegal
  bat is put in play (source: email {{leagueAbbrev}} - Discipline Expectations &
  Policy).
- Players must not throw bats or helmets, and must not intentionally hurt
  another player; intentionally injuring another player leads to immediate
  ejection, suspension from future games, and/or dismissal from the team
  (source: email {{leagueAbbrev}} - Discipline Expectations & Policy).
- Fighting, injuring other players, or acts beyond in-game discipline can
  result in immediate removal from the game with a discipline review by the
  Board of Directors, which may impose single or multi-game suspensions
  (source: email {{leagueAbbrev}} - Discipline Expectations & Policy).
- Player playing-up requests are decided case by case with the safety of the
  requesting player and other players as a primary criterion, generally
  following the Little League age charts (source: agenda 2024-02-04).

## 7. Team Equipment: Provision, Care & Security

The league buys and maintains shared team equipment (helmets, catcher's gear,
bats, and baseballs) and distributes it to the fields in locked bins.

Care standards for coaches and players (source: email {{leagueAbbrev}} - Baseball
Equipment - Please Read):

- Helmets should be placed on a line on the ground so they do not fill with
  dirt or get scratched.
- Catcher's gear must be kept in the dugout and out of the rain, hung on
  dugout hooks where available.
- Bats must be returned to the bat rack and kept organized during games and
  practices.
- Each field is stocked with two buckets of roughly 48 baseballs (about 96
  total, numbered in groups). Coaches must leave five minutes at the end of
  practice to sort and recover balls, and report any that cannot be found so
  they can be replaced before the next practice (source: email {{leagueAbbrev}} -
  Baseball Equipment - Please Read).

Security of equipment is a principal duty of the head coach. It is the head
coach's last responsibility on leaving the field to make absolutely sure the
bins are locked and the balls are put back in the bins (source: email {{leagueAbbrev}} - Equipment). This standard exists because leaving bins open has resulted in
stolen baseballs and gear soaked by rain, and there has been repeated theft
of baseballs (source: email {{leagueAbbrev}} - Equipment; source: agenda 2025-02-24).

Bin access and distribution:

- Equipment is stored in coded/locked bins and clubhouses distributed by
  field; access codes are for adults only and are not to be shared, as theft
  has occurred in prior years (source: agenda 2025-02-24; source: email {{leagueAbbrev}} - Combinations to field bins).
- Where a field lacks its own gear, teams share equipment with a neighboring
  field per the Tee Ball/Coach Pitch VP's assignments (source: email {{leagueAbbrev}} - Combinations to field bins).
- The board's contingency plan for continued bin theft is to move equipment
  into the snack shack (source: agenda 2025-02-24).
- Equipment is prepared and sorted before the season (typically staged at
  Douglas) and distributed to fields by set (Tee Ball sets, AA/AAA/Majors
  sets) by the Equipment Manager and volunteers (source: email Equipment Prep;
  source: agenda 2022-03-16).

Equipment roles and purchasing authority:

- The board maintains an Equipment Manager position filled annually
  (source: agenda 2024-02-04). An "equipment team" volunteer role is available
  at all levels to prepare and distribute gear (source: email Equipment Team
  Volunteer Role).
- Equipment purchases are authorized by board motion. The board approved up to
  {{amount}} for 2023-season equipment (source: agenda 2023-02-26) and again
  moved {{amount}} for new equipment for 2025 at the discretion of the president
  and equipment manager (source: agenda 2025-02-24).
- The 2025 inventory targets included roughly 24 helmets for tee ball/player
  pitch, 12 helmets each for Majors/Minors baseball and Minors/Majors
  softball; catcher's gear for player pitch and minor/major levels; about
  20 dozen practice and 30 dozen season baseballs; and a dozen each of
  baseball and softball bats (source: agenda 2025-02-24).
- The league receives an annual {{equipmentVendor}} sponsorship equipment
  credit (for example, {{amount}} for 2025) redeemed with a league partner discount
  code (source: email {{leagueName}} Equipment Credit
  Info).

## 8. Uniforms

- Uniforms are ordered through the league's chosen vendor(s) ({{uniformVendor}} for
  jerseys and hats in recent seasons); softball uniforms may be ordered
  separately from a site the softball program chooses (source: agenda
  2025-02-24; source: agenda 2024-03-03).
- The base uniform budget covers a jersey, pants, and hat per player, with
  sponsor names combined with the league logo on the front and player names
  and numbers on the back (source: agenda 2025-02-24; source: agenda
  2023-02-26).
- There is a firm final registration deadline to receive a customized jersey
  (for example, March 9 for 2023/2024 and March 14 for 2025), with no
  exceptions (source: agenda 2024-02-04; source: agenda 2025-02-24).
- Head coaches may receive a matching coach jersey free, with a recommended
  {{amount}} donation to offset the cost, and family/fan matching jerseys may be
  offered as a fundraiser at roughly {{amount}} over cost requiring up-front payment
  (source: agenda 2023-02-26).
- Placing a business name or logo on the kids' uniform shirts is handled as a
  sponsorship; inquiries are routed to the Sponsorship Coordinator
  (source: email T shirt logo question).
- A Uniform Manager (and uniform team) is a designated staff role responsible
  for ordering, logo selection, and distribution (source: agenda 2023-02-26;
  source: agenda 2024-03-03).
